
What _is_ corporate finance? (in one sentence)

**Corporate finance is the study of how companies create, measure, and distribute value under uncertainty.**

Not accounting.  
Not stock picking.  
Not budgeting.

👉 It’s **decision-making under risk with money over time**.



At its core, **corporate finance answers 3 questions**:

1. Where should the company invest?
2. How should it pay for those investments?
3. How should value be returned to owners?

Corporate finance exists to **protect companies from bad intuition**.

The ONE idea everything rests on (super important)

Before any subjects, here is the **axiom** of corporate finance:

> **Value = cash flows × timing × risk**

Everything you will learn explains **one of these three**.

Everything fits into **one loop**:

> **Business → Cash flows → Risk → Value → Price → Decision**

# PHASE 1 — Financial statements (the raw material)

**Theory:** Accounting → adjusted for finance

Cash flow shows when value is actually transferred, not just reported. Accounting can lie as profits include revenues not yet collected, expenses not yet paid.

Key object of analysis: FCF (free cash flow), it is the cash the business can distribute without harming operations.

Two companies:
- Company A: High profit, negative cash flow
- Company B: Low profit, strong cash flow

By valuation theory, Company **B** is more valuable **because**:

> **Value comes from distributable cash, not reported profit.**

Mental rules:
**Cash explains truth**  
**ROIC explains quality**  
**WACC explains value** WACC (Weighted Average Cost of Capital)

Value creation = ROIC − WACC

- If ROIC > WACC → growth creates value
- If ROIC < WACC → growth destroys value
Only ROIC > WACC ensures **profitable reinvestment**. Even small positive spreads matter over time due to compounding.
