# The Book in 3 Sentences

Randomness, outliers and uncertainty

What's a black swan? Rare, huge impact, retrospective predictability. 

Low predictability plus huge impact is concerning, but humans tend to ignore them as they don't exist. That tells how blind we are to randomness.  

# Top Quotes
Why do we tend to see the pennies instead of the dollars?

# Summary & Reflection

A black swan is an event with 3 attributes:
1. It is an outlier, rare and unexpected.
2. It carries huge impact.
3. Humans always want to explain, rationalize or theorize it after happening, even though it's an outlier.

The author despises the bell curve method a lot, as he thinks the bell curve ignores large deviations, yet makes us confident that we tamed uncertainty.

Being a fool in the right place. This is the advice from author, be humble. Do not try to avoid predicting, the idea is not stopping being a fool, but to be a fool in the right places. What should be avoided is unnecessary dependence on large-scale harmful predictions, such as economic forecasts, however be fooled in small matters, do your own prediction on your next picnic.

Knowing that you cannot predict doesn't mean that you cannot benefit from unpredictability, the bottom line is: be prepared, for all relevant eventualities. Chance favours the prepared.
