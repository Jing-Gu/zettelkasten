# Summary & Reflection

## Optimal stopping 

Knowing when to stop

Lots of life decisions can boil down to the cs algorithm optimal stopping, such as looking for new house or selling the house, job hunting (from both applicants and HR angle), looking for parking, even dating. We constantly face the choice there, are we continuing or stopping? What about the next? Next may not be better, there is time cost or any cost, knowing when to stop is the key.

The math tells that the optimal stopping is 37%, before that threshold commit nothing just collect data, once pass it, take actions quickly, this is also called **Look and leap**.

## Explore / Exploit trade-off

Explore something new or stick with our favourite?

Our decisions are affected by the interval, where we are in the event or the life. Facing the same choice where to eat, we might easily try a new restaurant (explore) if we just move in this city, but we might go to the known and tried restaurant (exploit) if we are soon leaving this city tomorrow.

In CS, **exploration** is gathering info, and **exploitation** is using the info you have to get a known good result. 

Note that the value of **exploration** can only go down over time, as the remaining opportunities to savoir it dwindle. On the other side, the value of **exploitation** can only go up over time.

It seems that we tend to almost by instinct explore when we know we will stay for long, our young selves will explore so much more than older selves, aren't babies the most capable in explorations? Besides some algorithms such as the Gittins index, I feel more related to the mental framework mentioned by Jeff Bezos: a life with minimal regret. Project oneself into his 80s and think back, if we are doing can minimize the regret? For him the decision making is incredibly easy once the regret is clear.
