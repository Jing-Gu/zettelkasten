# Summary & Reflection

## Feedback loops
Simply put, a system's output becomes its input again to change its behaviour, to refine and improve.

Two types of feedback loops: **Balancing** and **reinforcing**, aka Negative and Positive.

The civilization is the result of feedback loops, as the civilization is the stream of approvals and disapprovals we all provide when we respond to people's conduct around us. That stream of approvals and disapprovals create feedback loops to encourage good behaviours and discourage bad behaviours. 

Feedback loops are a critical model as they are part of the life everyday whether you are aware of them or not. Without feedbacks, a system just do the same thing over and over. Understand and use them wisely to know where to direct the system changes.

## Equilibrium
Equilibrium is a state of balance. A system at equilibrium is in a stable state, in its comfortable zone, not to say totally static standstill, but more about dynamic equilibrium. It's the system's own way to self correct through feedback loops to nudge things that drift too far back to its rhythm.

**Homeostasis**, a dynamic equilibrium, first appeared to express the human body as a whole system is amazingly capable to maintain its internal state in response to its ever-changing external environment. 

Systems are rarely static, but continuously adjusting toward equilibrium. We can never reach an equilibrium in one shot that easy, don't think in a way that if we do X, then Y will happen (e.g. If we move, we'll be happy), as things always move in flux, we don't reach a certain steady state then stay forever. The life is made by endless adjustments. 

## Bottlenecks
Bottlenecks are the **limiting factors**, the slowest parts of a system. Identify the bottleneck or end up solving the wrong problem. Speed up the slowest part, the whole system will be fast, do not waste time optimizing parts that are already fast, they are not limiting factors.

Bottleneck is not equal to a **constraint**, which is a fundamental limitation of a system. A bottleneck can be anticipated and alleviated.

Bottleneck is not totally a villain, it can indeed inspire innovations. Tons of innovations were invented during wartime due to the material limitations. The material shortage was truly a bottleneck, when silk support was slow due to country conflicts, Nylon was invented and even more versatile. 

## Randomness

This might be the hardest model to use because humans are bad at understanding true randomness. We tend to see orders, want to figure out patterns, sequences, and hopefully to predict the next.

Randomness is challenging, as accepting it means that the universe is less friendly and comprehensible than we wish. Accepting randomness is to tell us that our lives is chance, and we don't have control. 

Past was as random as the future will be. History looks ordered only at the hindsight, humans tend to create a narrative to order and organize the world, makes us predictable, and this can be a weakness to exploit. Ask anyone to name a random vegetable or choose a random number, it is not really random, chances for those random things are not equal, people tend to follow the pattern, just like most random chosen veggie are carrots or broccolis, people don't realize that we are not making random choices. Generating true randomness is hard, that requires to find method that overrides the way our brain works.

Learning randomness help us to less predictable and more creative.

Randomness is the chaos that underlies the universe, it is unpredictable, uncontrollable, no patterns to follow, yet humans are terrible at recognizing it. We just want to see patterns even there are non, so we attribute meanings to coincidence, we think we can beat the odds. 

This mental model reminds me of the shooter and farmer hypothesis from the book Three body and [[The black swan]].


## Pareto principle
aka. 80/20 principle
Inputs and outputs are not equally distributed, not all items on a list are equally important, usually just a few of them are more important, somehow the split is always close to 80 - 20. Remember this principle can teach us where to focus time and energy.

## Regression to the mean
Extreme outcomes tend to be followed by more average outcomes. Either success or failure is usually followed by a result closer to the mean, not another extreme. Be careful to tell the luck from the skill, luck happens randomly once, but repeated iterations will cancel out those luck noise over time. 

This rule seems fit into every aspect of life, may that's why life has its ebb and flow. From a bigger perspective, regression to the mean is the universe's way to see "not so fast", extreme outcomes are rarely repeated, the universe somehow just keeps things in check, pull the exceptional back to the ordinary.
